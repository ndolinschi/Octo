THE decisive SWE number: under the benchmark's own cost model even the ORACLE saves 0.8 % [-7.2, +8.1] of the
always-high bill on coding trajectories. Its cost model is cache-aware (all prompt tokens are cache reads or cache
writes; a tier switch is a cold start that writes the whole prefix at the new tier, and switching back to high
writes it again at $6.25/M). Coding trajectories have long, growing prefixes; half of their steps need high; so the
cache write on every switch eats what the cheap steps save. The 76.5 % oracle saving of 3a comes from the
single-step workloads (mtRAG, QMSum, BFCL), not from agentic coding. Caveat: the bank's SWE trajectories are short
(8.4 routed steps; median 11 messages), and the cost model's caching rules are the benchmark's, not a provider's
invoice; with longer trajectories and sticky routing (switch rarely) the picture could differ - not measured.

3e. What a real step-level router would need (stage3_gencost.json; arithmetic with the benchmark's own algorithm,
prices and cost model; NO API was called).
  Algorithm (docs/DATA_GENERATION.md): an always-high seed trajectory; then for each routed step, earlier steps
  locked, try candidate tiers from the cheapest (up to 3 models per tier), with every LATER step at high, run to the
  end and evaluate. Trials per step, assumed: gold low 2, mid 3+2, mid_high 6+2, high 9; SWE gold mix -> 6.5 trials
  per step on average. Each trial costs the always-high suffix from that step.
  On the bank's own SWE trajectories (8.4 routed steps): seed $0.092, search $2.96, total $3.05 per task
  (= 33x the seed); all 520 trajectories of the bank: $195 (SWE $122, PinchBench $25, BFCL $20, QMSum $15, mtRAG $13).
  These trajectories are far shorter than real mini-SWE-agent runs. Real Opus runs in the core: $0.55 (opus-4.6) to
  $0.75 (opus-4.5 high) per task, 23-29 calls median. Search cost grows ~quadratically with length: ratio ~= trials
  per step (6.5) x steps (n) x mean suffix share (0.59 on the bank) -> n = 25-30: ~100-115x the seed -> about
  $55-85 per task, plus Docker evaluation of ~6.5 x n ~= 160-200 runs per task (not priced).
  How many tasks: stage 2 and 3c show a step model on 40 SWE tasks reaches accuracy 0.67 vs 0.50 and still cannot
  turn it into savings; SWE-Router-style learned detectors use thousands of trajectories. An order of 500-2 000 SWE
  tasks is a plausible minimum for a learned step router: $1.5k-6k at the bank's short-trajectory cost model, about
  $30k-170k at realistic trajectory lengths (at the benchmark's listed prices, before evaluation compute). And per
  3c, the cost model itself says per-step switching on SWE saves ~nothing even with perfect labels, so a "sticky"
  label (switch for a whole phase, not a step) would have to be defined first.

room is larger between concrete models (+0.03 to +0.05 noise-bounded) than between price tiers (+0.017), so "there
is almost nothing to predict" was too strong; "there is a small amount, and nothing available predicts it" holds:
routing-time text gives AUROC <= 0.55 for "cheap fails and dear solves" and ~0 area; early trajectories detect
cheap failure weakly but not the useful event, and the honest cascade gains 0 to +0.01. The largest effect is again
model selection: within each declared ladder one model often dominates another on cost per task (T1, T4).
