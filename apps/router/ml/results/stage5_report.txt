STAGE 5 REPORT - does a ladder that learns online beat a ladder read off a public leaderboard when the router's work differs?
Date 2026-11-13. Location: apps/router/ml/. Every number below is in results/stage5_xbench.json (make xbench, ~3 min).
New: src/routerml/{xbench_fetch,xbench_data,stage5}.py, tests/test_stage5.py (31 tests), Makefile targets stage5 /
xbench-fetch / xbench, README lines, figures/stage5_{rank_scatter,reward_by_policy,learner_vs_ladder}.png. Stages 1-4 code
and numbers untouched (config.py only gained stage 5 constants). 64 tests pass. No paid API, no git writes.
Seeds config.SEED + 500..530. Intervals 95 %: percentiles over 200 streams, or 1000 two-sided task bootstraps (correlations).

HYPOTHESIS. A learned ladder can beat a leaderboard ladder only when the router's own work ranks models differently from
the leaderboard. Test: SWE-bench Verified (stage 4's 33 mini-SWE-agent arms) = "the leaderboard"; another benchmark =
"the router's own traffic".

ONE-LINE ANSWER. Yes, but only when the leaderboard's pick is clearly wrong on the target, and that happened in one of
our two targets. On Terminal-Bench 2 (shell and system tasks, other scaffolds) the Verified leader ranks 4th of 5 under
one scaffold. There every learner beats the leaderboard ladder from the first decision: +0.09 [+0.03, +0.14] solved per
task in a single pass over the 89 real tasks (ts_src20, quality-only). But so does a uniformly random choice
(+0.05 [+0.01, +0.10]). On SWE-bench Multilingual (other languages, the same scaffold) the ranking transfers (Spearman
0.86) and the leaderboard ladder wins at every V. In neither target does a learner reach a leaderboard measured on the
target itself (held-out half).

3. THE DECISIVE EXPERIMENT (figures stage5_reward_by_policy.png, stage5_learner_vs_ladder.png)
======================================================================================================
Setup:
  * Bandit feedback: per decision one model; the environment returns one random trial of that cell, and nothing else.
    Tested: flipping every unchosen cell leaves all decisions identical.
  * Scores are the expected reward of the chosen cell: p - lambda x mean cost.
  * Leaderboard ladder: the matched model with the best Verified aggregate (solve rate - lambda x mean cost on
    Verified), used unchanged.
  * Held-out half: per stream a random half A of the target's tasks; its best model is used on half B. Tested: it
    never reads B, and the split streams never contain A.
  * Learners as in stage 4: ts_flat; ts_src{n0} (Beta prior of strength n0 from the Verified rate, plus one cost
    pseudo-observation at the Verified mean cost); eps-greedy; forgetting with discount 0.99; plus uniform random as a
    reference.
  * Streams:
    - one pass = the real target tasks once (89 / 88 / 300);
    - 500 and 2000 = tasks resampled with replacement. Resampling the same 89 tasks overstates how much future work
      resembles past work, so treat those columns as optimistic for every learner;
    - split = one pass over half B (44 tasks; 150 on Multilingual), and 500 resampled from B.

3c. Dependence on n0 (strength of the Verified prior):
  * When the leaderboard is wrong (TB2), a stronger prior costs a little in the long run: Terminus2 V=inf at 2000,
    flat +0.185 vs n0=50 +0.166. On short horizons it helps: tb2_all one pass, n0=50 +0.019 vs flat +0.004; overtake
    after 1 decision vs 71.
  * When the leaderboard is right (Multilingual), a stronger prior loses less: V=$1 one pass, n0=50 -0.077 vs n0=5
    -0.128 vs flat -0.079 (flat is helped by its cost prior). It never turns into a win.
  * The informed learner is the safer default, but n0 does not change the sign of any result.

3d. Reverse control: traffic = Verified (500 tasks, matched Terminus 2 models), leaderboard = TB2 Terminus 2 aggregate.
  * V=inf: the TB2 ladder picks opus-4.6 (0.756 on Verified vs best 0.758, regret 0.002). Learners lose: ts_flat
    -0.011 [-0.028,+0.004], ts_src20 -0.004 [-0.017,+0.002] at 2000. Uniform loses: -0.026.
  * Cost-aware V: the TB2 ladder picks deepseek-v3.2, which is cheap under Terminus 2 but $0.45 per task under

    ts_src20 +0.432 of a possible +0.434.
  * So the direction holds both ways: whenever the source ladder is wrong on the traffic by more than a few points,
    learning on the traffic wins. When it is right, learning loses a little.

Break-even, stated in the quantity that matters: the leaderboard ladder's own per-task regret on the traffic.
  * Within 2000 decisions: the learner wins on average once that regret exceeds about 0.01-0.03 solved per task
    (quality-only).
  * Within 500 decisions: about 0.03-0.05.
  * Within a single pass of ~90-300 real tasks: about 0.04-0.09.
  * In rank terms: Spearman below about 0.5-0.6 between leaderboard and traffic. Rank correlation alone is a poor
    guide, because what matters is whether the top pick changes and by how much.
  * Measured regret against these thresholds: TB2 (0.17-0.20) is far above every threshold. Multilingual (0.02 at
    V=inf, 0.037 at $20, 0 at cost-first V) is at or below the long-horizon threshold, and the measured result agrees:
    a tie to a small loss.

======================================================================================================
5. SYNTHESIS
======================================================================================================
Does online learning of the ladder beat a leaderboard-based ladder under distribution shift between benchmarks?

Only when the leaderboard's pick is wrong on the router's work by more than a few points of solve rate.
  * Terminal-Bench 2 (shell and system tasks, other scaffolds): the Verified leader drops from rank 1 to rank 4 of 5
    under one scaffold, and from 3 to 8 of 11 over all scaffolds. The leaderboard ladder's regret is 0.17-0.20 solved
    per task. Every learner beats it: +0.09 [+0.03, +0.14] in one pass of the 89 real tasks (Terminus 2), and +0.12 to
    +0.18 on long resampled streams.
  * SWE-bench Multilingual (other languages, the same scaffold): the ranking transfers (Spearman 0.86, regret
    0-0.04). The learners lose by 0.01-0.08 per task.
  * Three qualifications:
    (1) On Terminus 2 a uniformly random choice also beats the ladder (+0.05), so part of the "win" only says that
        the leaderboard picked one of the worst models.
    (2) On the pooled TB2 target, where uniform loses, the learner's gain over 88 real tasks is not distinguishable
        from 0 (+0.004 to +0.019). It becomes clear only on resampled streams of 500+, which overstate repetition.
    (3) In no setting does a learner come close to a leaderboard measured on the target itself. A held-out half of
        the target's tasks gives +0.17 to +0.20 over the Verified ladder on TB2; the learners get +0.08 on the same
        44 tasks.
    Benchmarking candidate models on a sample of one's own work beats learning online from scratch.

The sentence the thesis may write:
"With public data, an online-learned model ladder beats a ladder read off SWE-bench Verified only when the target
workload ranks the models differently. On Terminal-Bench 2, where the Verified leader falls from 1st to 4th of 5, a
Thompson-sampling ladder with a leaderboard prior gains 0.09 [0.03, 0.14] solved tasks per task over 89 tasks (0.18 on
long streams). On SWE-bench Multilingual, where the ranking transfers (Spearman 0.86), it loses 0.01-0.08 per task. It
pays when the leaderboard's pick is wrong by more than about 0.03-0.05 solved per task at 500 decisions (0.01-0.03 at
2000). A small offline benchmark on a sample of the router's own tasks beats both."

What it would take on the owner's router:
  * Each session needs an outcome proxy: the last test run (session_outcomes already parses cargo, pytest and
    unittest summaries), accept or rewind in the harness, or a follow-up correction. It also needs the served model,
    the cost, and the decision probabilities.
  * Detecting a leaderboard error of the TB2 size (0.2 solved per task) needs about 50-100 sessions with an outcome
    per candidate model (two-proportion test at p ~ 0.5: n ~ 2(1.96+0.84)^2 x 0.25 / 0.2^2 ~ 50 per arm).
  * An error of the size that matters at the break-even (0.03-0.05) needs about 800-2 000 sessions per arm, i.e.
    several thousand labelled sessions over 4-5 arms, and more if the proxy is noisy. A proxy that agrees with the true
    outcome a fraction q of the time shrinks the effect by (2q - 1), so q = 0.8 needs about 2.8x as many sessions.
  * The cheaper first step: replay a fixed sample of 50-100 of the owner's own past tasks on each candidate model,
    offline. That is the held-out-half baseline, the best policy measured here.

======================================================================================================
NOT VERIFIED / LIMITS / LEFT OPEN
======================================================================================================
- Target size: 89 TB2 tasks and 5 matched models under one scaffold. The correlation intervals are wide (Spearman
  0.40 [-0.10, 0.90]).
- Resampled 500 and 2000 streams reuse the same tasks: optimistic for every learner, as stated.
- TB2 "model" = model + scaffold + serving. minimax and kimi ran on ollama cloud endpoints in Terminus 2, and their
  cost is imputed (tokens x list price, an upper bound). glm-5 has no cost at all.
- tb2_all cost is mostly imputed medians, so its cost-aware results (tb2_all_costed in the JSON) are not reliable and
  are not used in the text above.
- Dropping infrastructure-error trials (rather than counting them as 0, as the official leaderboard does) changes
  per-system rates. Not re-run the other way.
- Multilingual has one run per cell. On Verified the core arms of a few models (gemini-3-pro, mini v1.15) are older
  versions than the Multilingual runs (v2.0.0a0).
- SWE-bench full `test` restricted to repositories outside Verified was NOT built: its 24 submissions are 2023-2025
  models, almost none in the Verified core.
- SWE-rebench / SWE-bench-Live per-model per-instance data: not checked (left open).
- The learners' hyper-parameters are stage 4's, unchanged. Policies were not tuned on target outcomes. The
  break-even was measured on the targets' own rates (a simulation, not an outcome).
- No live router data: the session-count estimate above is a power calculation, not a measurement.
